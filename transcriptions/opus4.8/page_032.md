**Interpretive note.** A continuation of the merchant's units-of-sale glossary, classifying goods by the measure or count in which they are traded. Sold by the *cantaro grosso* (144 lb): bulk raw materials and chemicals — lead of Venice, iron, sulphur of Pisa/Naples/Seville, dates, soft and hard soap, flax, sinopia (red ochre), and cheeses of Sardinia and Castile. Sold by the dozen: skins and leathers — sheepskins (*boldroni*), cordovans, parchment, Parisian and Flemish skins, fish-glue. Sold by the piece: Florentine and overseas cloths, white/black fustians, Spanish satins, veils, cottons, and furs (sables, martens, vair, wild cats). By the bale: linens of Champagne and Montpellier and dyed greens of Savona; by the hundred: crossbow stocks, ox-hides, Venice planks, and fir lance-shafts; and further classes "by the gaffio" and "by weight." Recurring weight tags ("pesa 8¼ di Firenze", "pesa lb 40") tie the goods to Florentine weight standards. The very small, heavily abbreviated hand leaves many commodity names uncertain.
