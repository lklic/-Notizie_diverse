**English translation.**

**+ Barcelona**

- Sell ("mětti") at Barcelona spun gold ("oro filato") wholesale; for each pound (lb) you spend, the Genoa pound is returned — the Genoa pound equals nine ounces (on) at Barcelona.
- Bring ("trasi") to Barcelona unlimited lume/tin ("lome"), sheepskins ("boldroni"), cotton-cloth ("bambagina"), lambskins ("agnine"), alum ("aludi"), dyes/grains ("rani"), much saffron ("zafferano"), honey ("mele"), dates ("dattery"), coarse cloths ("panj grossi"), worked and unworked corals.
- Sell at Barcelona spices ("spezie") of every kind.
- Exporting unlimited goods: much spice, spun gold thread, torzonelli (twisted thread), lead/tin ("piombi"), cloths, fustians, and *marino* — they go to *innolano* (Milan?).
- One obtains there cottons and linens of every kind, white re-..., taffeta of Bologna, and *Cale* (calicut linen?) whole, of every type.
- Bring from Monolif(?) lume/tin; and they ship out hose-cloths ("panj calzonto").

**+ Catalonia**

- Catalonia weighs everything ("Calonza filonda agnj rosa") by the Barcelona standard: 11 Barcelona [pounds] equal the pound of Aragon.
- One sells silver "argentini" of the *l'orto cintore* standard: 120 lb of Catalonia returns 137 lb at Genoa.
- One sells of another *l'orto cintore* standard: it returns at Genoa 15 ounces (on).
- One sells the "ottavo" type at 36 (gelti); *tarchaltea* of zinzola at 36.
- One sells Barbary silver "argentini" at 144; returns at Genoa lb 162 and 164. The *l'orto cintore* standard of Catalonia: one "grossa" returns at Genoa lb 400. The linens *monodoni* correspond to all the *gealli* and make at Genoa [...] ounces; at Palermo [...] and Bologna.
- One sells every kind by the *gavurde* standard — by these gross *haverse* (avers/wares), the *Calvo* lume, and the *Calzime* coarse standard.
- The large quintal ("contare grosso") weighs cloth, lead, fish ("firs"=pesci?), salt, and other goods.
- One sells gold spun wholesale; for each pound spent the [Genoa] pound is returned — 8 of Genoa for 10 of Catalonia in ounces.
- One sells refined silver ("avemto amarisco"=argento) by the cantar per the Barcelona standard; in March, 8 ounces returns *alqgga* 7½ ounces; fine, 5½ of Catalonia make at Genoa 60 ounces.
- Bring to Catalonia: grain ("grome"=grano?), lume/tin, glass ("vetro"), almonds ("mandrele"), honey ("mele"), lambskins ("agnine"), sheepskins ("boldroni"), woad/dye in grain ("bavinno i(n) grana"), cloth, hazelnuts ("nodola"=nocciole), goods of every kind ("chosa dõny"), wheat ("faro"=farro), *barghino*, *dõmo*, dates ("dattery"), rice ("risj"), *gonza*, and cloths.
- A cafisso ("chassisso") of grain at Catalonia returns at Florence 8 *prana* and a half / mr 30.
- One obtains spices of every kind, much coral, cottons, linens, fustians, *garbi*, iron ("ferro"), and *marino*, which go to *innolano* (Milan?).
- Exporting unlimited goods of every kind: cottons, gold, and spun silver and torzonelli.
- One obtains white *grosana* cloth, taffeta of Bologna, lead, whole salt ("sale inteo"), and cloths of Florence — the end ("fim").
- ① They are stamped/sealed ("zombransi") properly with their *gostifa*(?) money at Barcelona.

---

**Interpretive note.** A paired gazetteer of the western markets of Barcelona ("Barzalona") and Catalonia ("Calonza"), listing for each what a Florentine merchant should sell, import, and export, with the chief commodities (spun gold, silver of various assay standards, spices, corals, cottons, fustians, sheepskins, alum, saffron, honey, dates, grain) and the weight ragguagli (ratios) linking the Catalan/Barcelona pound to the Genoese pound and ounce, with cross-references to Florence, Bologna, Palermo, and an unidentified "innolano" (likely Milan). The Catalonia block is especially rich in silver-assay terms ("argentini", "l'orto cintore", "ottavo", "barbarescho") and their conversions to Genoa. Several technical terms remain uncertain due to abrasion and are flagged.
