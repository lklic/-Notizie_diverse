**English translation.**

> **+ [Brief?] of how payments are made, and [goods] bartered or sold**

- **Montpellier** — exchange is reckoned against [the rate of] France; the *escudo* (?) of gold is worth ... ; at 125 [it is] better to hold (when?) selling, according to the season.
- And from Montpellier, selling, the exchange is reckoned against [the local rate]; they are better, by price, according to the season.
- **Barcelona, Valencia and all Catalonia** — exchange is reckoned against the Barcelona [standard].
- And from those same places, selling, the exchange is reckoned against [the standard].
- **Seville (Cibilia) and all Spain** — exchange [reckons] so many per [unit] when selling, against one *dobla* (gold coin).
- **Bruges** — exchange is reckoned thus against so many *grossi*; and likewise from Bruges, so many *grossi* ... thus.
- **London and England** — exchange is reckoned thus against so many *sterlini* (sterling pence).
- And from those same places, selling, exchange [gives] so many sterling against the [unit].
- Sterling 32 to 35 to the lb, according to the season.
- **Paris** — exchange is reckoned against *francs*; one can always [trade them?] — better when [selling] some quantity, as the price often varies.
- And from Paris, selling, the exchange is reckoned against [the standard].

**Interpretive note.** A Genoa-centred *prattica di mercatura* page. The upper block explains in what unit and against which currency/standard spices and goods are exchanged, bought and sold in Montpellier, Barcelona/Valencia/all Catalonia, Seville/Spain, Bruges, London/England (in *sterlini*) and Paris (in *franchi*), all "according to the season." The lower block is a metrological gazetteer of **Genoa**: it defines the Genoese fine and gross *centinaio*/*migliaio* in libbre (gross centinaio = lb 157 fine, migliaio = lb 1570), then converts lb 100 of Genoa fine into the pound of some sixteen marketplaces from Bologna to Geneva, notes saffron of Barletta, gives a silver/gold-mark assay of Genoa against Florence, Genoa and Bologna, and closes with notes on selling Levant vs. Western goods at Venice and a Genoese export duty (*magattera*).
