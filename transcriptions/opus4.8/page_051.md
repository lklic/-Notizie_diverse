**English translation.**

**✠ Alexandria, continued.**

- ※ In Alexandria they sell certain fine goods / by *sotraggio*(?) ...; one good ounce (*onna*) turns into one ounce in Genoa.
- ※ All goods are sold in Alexandria absent (?) ...; they last 1 ⅛ ...; according to the merchant's needs ... and 98(?) *onchoresi* (mithqal) make one *besante* (bezant).
- ☞ And the other(?) of silver turns in Alexandria into 127 *onchoresi*-weights.
- ☞ Gold is sold *a botte* (?) in Alexandria by the *grossi* account; ¼ is deducted from each *botte*(?).
- ※ Fruit is bought/sold by weight (*a peso*) ... 2(?) *querni*.
- ☞ A *cantara* of 10 ⅛(?) *querni* equals one ... of fruit from Naples.
- ✠ **Exported (*trasi*) from Alexandria:** cottons / abundant spices / silk cloths / gold florins / ...(?) / pearls / and other coarse goods.

**Interpretive note.** This folio (25r) gives a two-part trade gazetteer for the Levant. The top block finishes the Alexandria (Egypt) section, recording local weight/coin equivalences — the silver weight converting to 127 *onchoresi* (mithqal), the *mithqal*/*besante* (bezant) ratio, gold and fruit allowances, and the *cantara* — then lists the goods exported from Alexandria (cottons, abundant spices, silk cloths, gold florins, pearls) and imported into it (Florentine, Lombard and Lucchese cloths, oil, almonds, saffron, chestnuts, copper, tin, orpiment, refined silver, saltpetre). The second block, "Damascho" (Damascus), is a structured commodity catalogue organised by the unit of sale (*argentora*, *rotolo*, *a romano*, *saggio*, *montanaro*, *moliario*, *a pezza*), enumerating the spices, drugs, dyestuffs, metals and cloths of the Damascus trade — ginger, cinnamon, brazilwood, sandalwood, camphor, alum, cloves, nutmeg, rhubarb, aloe, scammony, coral, saffron, amber, and the European woollens (Florentine, Brussels, Holland serge) imported in exchange. This is a classic *pratica della mercatura* market profile; the dense, abraded mercantesca hand makes many commodity names uncertain.
