**Interpretive note.** A merchant's reference glossary listing tradeable goods classified by the unit under which they are sold. It opens with weight conversions of the *libbra a balanza* (1 cantar = lb 360 by the balance, lb 300 at Barcelona, lb 375 at Florence, lb 4[2]5 at Venice, of the fine pound), then enumerates goods sold "in balanza": pepper, ginger, cinnamon, lac, refined sugars (of two and three *cotte*/boilings), brazilwood, frankincense, alums, gallnuts, woad (*guado*) by grade, cottons (Barbary, Alexandrian, Maltese, Turkish), wax, copper, tin, verdigris, orpiment, saltpetre, mastic, kermes-grain (*vermini di grana*) and dyed threads. Further rubrics cover goods sold "a libra grossa" (cloves, nutmeg, scammony, dragon's-blood, cardamom, aloes, cubebs, tutty, sandalwood, saffron, storax, silks of Almería, gold/silk thread), "a once" (spun gold of Genoa, Germany, Bologna, Lucca; amber) and "a libra per libre xxx" (Roman alum, kermes grana, oil). The hand is small, heavily abbreviated and abraded, so many individual commodity names remain uncertain.
