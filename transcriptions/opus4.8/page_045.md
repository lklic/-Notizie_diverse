**English translation.**

**✠ Milan.** Milan(-goods)[?] are sold: alum is understood at f(lorins) of 32; sold by silver, by "hot" (calde). — And 100 lb of Milan convert to 98 lb in Florence. — Silver is sold by the marco, all fine; and gold "in grain"[?] at 8 oz d. 10. — Sterlings (sterlini) are sold "acipeza", "a tornesi", per florins of 32 a florin. — At Milan almost all the goods of all Lombardy are gathered, to be put "in grain"/wholesale[?]. — From there one draws endless mercery of every kind, and armor, of [...]; iron and "morali"[?], fustians, linen-cloth, much fine cloth. — From Como (Comò) much fine cloth is drawn. — From Monza[?] coarse, finished cloth is drawn; it is sent on toward Germany (Alemagna) and the Levant. — From Bologna (Abonona) cloth of every kind is drawn, and likewise; and from "Monto da"[?] raw (cloth) is drawn from certain towns thereabouts, and many [...], and "anfora"[?]. — From Monferrato much saffron is drawn, and "candosino"[?] and "chamapo"[?]. — From Brescia[?] are drawn arms[?], iron, flax, saffron and [...].

**✠ Lisbon (Lismona).** At Lisbon "appuy agenota": [reckoned] at florins of the chamber f. 13, at florins "corrente" f. 13, "franzio" f. 13; the *scudo* of the duke of the chamber f. 34, current per [...]; the said reckoning of silver is grossi of "copa adverina"[?]; [for] linen by the pound. — Montpellier exchanges "affranzio" against "franzio"; at times the "guroli"[?] of Montpellier are better, [reckoned] in temporal persons[?]. — Barcelona (Borzalona) exchanges "affranzio", reckoned in so many Barcelonese soldi. — Paris (Parigi)[?] exchanges "affranzio", reckoned in "franzio". — Other places[?] [reckon by] the chamber[?], counted against franc/florin, "sarbonono", Italian lands[?]. — At Lisbon every merchandise [is reckoned] "affranzio corrente" at f. 24; at "grossi"[?] f. 12; at florins "corrente"; pounds of the chamber f. 13. — The "uuga"[?] of Avignon (Abigione) "dyntare" converts wholesale to 7 lb s. 84. — The *libbra* of Avignon "dyntare" converts wholesale to 16½ oz. — "corte abonbarga"[?] is sold by the gross "melisme" 16 "fontane", good coarse. — Linen is sold by "argorba", which gives a good "corda", wholesale at Rome s. 9½. — The *cantar* of Avignon is all one with that of Marseille and that of "avri"[?]. — Apples, "chamondula" and "avarosche"[?] are sold by the three *cantara*. — Now per [unit] turned out of Marseille they are of poor sale[?], exposed at Lisbon(?), 6 lb 21 "morgasta" 117 [...]. — "marigione"[?] is drawn from here, from "alto prudenza"[?], item firstly. — At Lisbon by the gross "dyntare" it converts wholesale to 7 lb s. 37. — At Lisbon at one "barensa" where there are "ter gentory"[?] it converts to fine[?] 7 lb s. 755 and 760. — At Lisbon pounds "ortunto amaverzo"[?] convert wholesale to 8 oz d. 13. — **One draws from Lisbon to Marseille and other lands of the country: "oucle"[?], almonds, oil, "roma"[?], coarse wool, "orgine"[?], cotton[?], much "chromo", cumin, "chayri" of spicery, and "balde"** [...]. — **One sends to Lisbon: much spicery of every kind, gold, spun silver, silk "zornali"[?], Bologna taffeta, fine "chayri", and "cariadure" (armor) of Milan.**

**Interpretive note.** A two-part market gazetteer. The **Melano (Milan)** block describes the Milan market — alum and silver "al marco," the weight equivalence (100 lb Milan = 98 lb Florence), gold sold "in grain," and a checklist of goods (mercery, armor, iron, fustians, fine cloth) drawn into Milan from the Lombard/Piedmontese feeder towns (Como, Monza[?], Bologna, Monferrato with saffron, Brescia[?]) for re-export toward Germany and the Levant. The **Lismona (Lisbon)** block records monetary reckonings (florins of the chamber and "corrente") and exchange usances linking Lisbon with Montpellier, Barcelona, Paris and Avignon, several weight/money conversions to wholesale and to Genoa/Marseille, and lists of import/export commodities — almonds, oil, wool, cotton, cumin, spicery, gold and silver thread, Bologna taffeta and Milanese armor. Many lines are very heavily abbreviated and are marked uncertain.
