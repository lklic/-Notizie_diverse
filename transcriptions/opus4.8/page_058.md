**English translation.**

*(Heading)* This [?] shall be noted: the law (legge) made in the year 1420 concerning foreign persons (forenore = forestieri) who wish to dwell in Pisa; and likewise concerning the merchandise that is brought in and out of Pisa, [which] shall be allowed to pass; and by the first chapter (capitolo) they ratified the said law on the 18th of November in the aforesaid year.

**1.** If any merchandise should be brought into Pisa from the Levant (Levante) or from the West (Ponente) — understanding by "Levant" Rome (Roma) and [points] from there southward, and by "West" Genoa (Genova) and [points] from there southward, or from any island, by sea by way of Calabria, [whether for] the Pisan harbor or [points] outside the Arno — and were to be carried out again by sea, or otherwise, within the year from its entry, to be sent (or were it sent) to Genoa, or [points] southward, or roundabout, or [points] northward, no customs duty (gabella) or toll (dazio) need be paid, either on entry or on export.

**2.** In another case, [if] the merchandise should change its owner (mutasse signora), the export duty (gabella dell'uscita) must be paid; and if within the year it were not taken out, or were taken out by sea — not by the open sea[?] between Genoa and Rome — or indeed were taken out by land, … to whatever place one pays both the entry duty and the export duty, as set out above in the present concession (permigione = permissione).

**3.** Furthermore, [if] any merchandise [is] brought into Pisa, by sea or by land, which were taken out of Pisa at any time to be conveyed into Lombardy (Lombardia), or which by transit had to pass through Lombardy to be conveyed into Germany (Allemagna) or into any other part of the world, no customs duty need be paid on its export.

**4.** Furthermore, [if] any merchandise should be brought into Pisa from Lombardy, or from another region by way of Lombardy, which were taken out of Pisa to set off within four months from its said entry into Pisa, to convey it into whatever part of the world, no customs duty or toll need be paid on entry or export.

**5.** And it is declared (demonsi = dicemonsi) that if it be taken out within the said four months, [one] must pay [only] half (la metà) of the duty for the entry, and on land that of the export; and if it were taken out by land during the four months, but thereafter, one must pay the whole duty, both of the entry and of the export.

**6.** And further, [as to whether] for the aforesaid goods, or some of those which are written above, in any part the [aforesaid] deduction is not understood, nor is given effect [or] benefit of any [kind] … and let them remain as at present they are.

**7.** Furthermore, [if] one should bring merchandise into Pisa which has a term in which it may be withdrawn — [under] some benefit one must give surety (sodare = guarantee) thereupon regarding the export duty — that merchandise, or likewise on its behalf, by sufficient sureties (mallevadori) who cannot be other [than these]: let them be either Florentines (fiorentini) or Pisans (pisani), present in Pisa, or to pay the due duty … should it have to, by any contingency (avvenimento) whether of time or of [circumstance].

**Interpretive note.** This page is not a commodity/exchange gazetteer but a copy of a Pisan statute (legge) of 1420, set out in seven numbered chapters, regulating the customs duty (gabella/dazio) on merchandise entering and leaving Pisa and on foreign merchants who settle there. The rules turn on the geographic origin/destination (Levante = Rome and southward; Ponente = Genoa and southward; islands via Calabria; goods entering/transiting via Lombardy bound for Germany or elsewhere), on whether goods change ownership (art. 2), and on time-limits (within the year; within four months, with a half-duty rebate, art. 5), and conclude (art. 7) with a surety requirement that guarantors (mallevadori) be Florentines or Pisans resident in Pisa. It reflects the Florentine commercial regime over Pisa after 1406.
