**English translation.**

*Upper rubric:* "+ Of this[?] ... concerning the quality (bontà) ... and purchase ... of merchandise[?] ..." (heavily abraded) — fol. 16.

- ※ Gold of Burgundy sells by the *oina/doina* of skeins (matassine), s. 12 and ¼ the *doina*, the larger one[?]; a *matassa* makes one little bundle (mazzetto) weighing half a *donna* (load), 15 "ghevoli"[?]; sold wholesale per *mazzo*... at s. 9 to 10½.
- ※ Cotton (cotone), dyed, of Mundargo[?], in single (zingole) skeins of 2 ounces in half-moon shape, sells at s. 9 the pound.
- ※ Cotton from "racha"[?] ought to be very fine and mixed, in "canes" (canoni)... cotton spun for common use sells at the going rate of the town; whatever is "bonificato"[?] the ounce, according to its fineness.

**Interpretive note.** Folio 16r has two parts. The short upper block concerns *oro filato* (spun/drawn gold thread) of Bologna and Burgundy, sold by the case/*doina* in *matasse* (skeins) and *mazzetti* (bundles) with weights in lb/once and prices in soldi, followed by two entries on dyed and Levantine fine *cotone* (cotton). The much longer lower block is a comparative assay (fineness) table of gold coinage of many mints — Florentine, Genoese, Sienese, Bolognese, Milanese, Paduan and papal florins; Hungarian and Roman ducats; English and Flemish nobles; reals of Spain and Portugal; doblas of Spain and Tunis — each rated for fineness in *charati* (carats out of 24, with the Florentine florin at the benchmark 24) and how much it is "better/worse" (*meglo*/*peggio*) by a `lb`/`affi(nato)` margin. Several coin epithets are heavily abbreviated and a few fractions trimmed at the right margin, so a number of readings are flagged uncertain.*
