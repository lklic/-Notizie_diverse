**English translation.**

- *(Heading)* The thing[s] after which they convert: in Florence and in Pisa they have, by the same weight... the gross/large [weight] and that of the commune of Florence.
- The *cantaro* of Genoa is [gross?]; every *rubbio* weighs 25 lb, and of *garbo* weight 150 lb should convert in Florence to 138 lb.
- The Sardinian *cantaro* is 8 *rubbi* of Genoa, which would make 200 lb of *garbo* weight, and converts in Florence to 180, [or] 184 lb.
- The *cantaro* of Naples is 25 *rubbi* of Genoa, which is 275 lb of *garbo* weight, and converts in Florence and Pisa to 250 lb.
- The *cantaro* of [Nîmes / "l'uomo"?] is 30 *rubbi* of Genoa; it converts in Florence to 110, [or] 125 lb. And 102 *libbre* of Lucca convert in Florence to 100 lb.
- [Grain]: from there it converts, accurately assayed, [at] 35¾ *sona* of Florence.
- A *salma* of grain there converts to 25¼ *sona* at... monte; [the other measure?] does not convert well.
- A *salma* there converts from Genoa to 2½ *ayme* of grain // ; by Florentine measure two *ayme* of grain convert from the *garbo* measure.
- A *salma* of barley there converts to... ½... of grain.
- A [measure] of grain of Rome is 8½ to 9 *sona* of Florence; reckon it 8¾ *sona*.
- 23 *harfissi*[?] of Seville, converted in Florence and by weight, [is] *staio* 25 of the Alfonsine... they come to 4¾ for me.
- Grain at Barletta... of every... is 7½ *salme*, which converts [to] three *moggia*, at the charge of *tratta* (export duty)... ½ its *sona* for the other charges.
- The *harfisso* of grain of Catalonia [Calonga] converts at Barcelona [to] two and a half *quartini*; and the said *harfisso* of Catalonia converts [to] eight and a half *sona* by the measure of Florence.
- The *staio* of [Arles, "dorli"] is larger than that of [Aigues-Mortes, "dasgnomonta"] by ⅕, that is: every hundred *sestieri* of Arles convert at Aigues-Mortes to one hundred [and twenty?] *sestieri*.
- The *rasiere* of grain of Cagliari [cassero] of Sardinia converts [to] a *mina* of garbo, [or] by the measure of Florence 3⅛ *sona*. The *rasiere* at Bonifacio [is]... 20 per...; two *tonchi*[?] of [grain] of Naples at Castellammare... and they are conformable to three *sona*.
- *(Textile/colour note)* ...*taffetà* of Bologna or of Florence... Catalonia, take note: ⅓ green / ⅓ green / ⅔ vermilion / of the whole piece 35, noted 35 lb; ⅔ vermilion / ⅓ vermilion / ⅔ green / 4 of *gengoly*[?]; 1 tawny and 1 black; ⅛ tawny / 1 black.
- Hides by the use of Florence should convert together [to] 104 lb.
- Hides by the use of [Domo]... can [be sold] at Ancona, by the same weight... of Florence.
- Hides by the use of Florence from [Ancona] which convert at Perugia / Siena / Gubbio / [Sgarrurero]... and by that measure 102 to 104 lb.
- Hides by the use of Bologna convert in Florence [to] 104 lb.
- Hides by the use of Seville convert in Florence [to] 190 lb, and every... Seville at the said weight... and... it weighs eight *once*; and this... would convert in Florence to about 7½ *once*.
- The *moggio* of salt... [from] Florence and Pisa to Barcelona is 90, [or] 80 *staia*, which is 4000 lb of salt, the *moggio* of Florence.
- The *argenuga*[?] of Genoa... lb forty[?] of Genoa converts in Florence to lb [one hundred?] thirty.

**Interpretive note.** A dense folio of *ragguagli* (mercantile equivalence tables) translating the commercial weights and dry measures of many marketplaces — Genoa (cantaro, rubbio, the "garbo" weight), Sardinia, Naples, Lucca, Rome, Seville (Sibilia), Barletta, Catalonia/Barcelona, Arles, Aigues-Mortes, Cagliari, Bonifacio, Bologna, Ancona/Perugia/Gubbio — into Florentine and Pisan weights and measures. It covers grain (grano) and barley (orzo) measures (salma, sona, staio, rasiere, moggio), salt, and hides (cuoia, "chibr"=cuoiame), with a short colour-proportion note (verde/vermiglio/biado/nero) for a piece of taffetà and an export-duty (tratta) reference. The lower third of the folio is blank.
