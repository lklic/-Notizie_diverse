**English translation.**

**13.** In our [court/land], that the ordinance which forbids that a claim cannot be made against foreigners shall not apply for that merchandise which is founded[?] … nor for goods passing in transit.

**14.** In our [land], that any foreigner who shall come … henceforth to dwell in Pisa[?] shall be exempt from every levy (*gravezza*) for the term of twenty years … nor [shall] he or his heirs[?] by this reason be liable to be charged, neither in person nor in goods nor in any other manner whatsoever, under penalty of [so many] lire.
— Concerning this, neither passage-toll nor the ordinary customs duties (*gabelle ordinarie*) are understood [to be included].

**15.** In our [land], besides this, [it shall be enforced/observed] for the said term of twenty years, for every debt, obligation or contract made at any time henceforth — dated the 25th of December in the year one thousand four hundred and fourteen (1414), at folio 33 of the first [book].

**16.** And in any place and for any reason, [by] writing or public instrument[?] drawn up, by this reason he shall not be molested under the aforesaid penalty, neither in person nor in goods.

**17.** And this is not to be understood for that which might arise[?] from any Florentine, or one subject to the Comune of Florence.

**18.** And all the aforesaid things are to be understood and observed in good faith … and [this is] all confirmed[?] solemnly through the appropriate councils of the people and Comune of Florence.

---

**Interpretive note.** This page is not a commodity/ragguaglio table but a copied legal text: numbered statute clauses (13–18) granting foreign merchants who come to dwell — apparently in **Pisa** — a twenty-year exemption from levies (*gravezze*) and protection of person and goods against claims for prior debts, obligations or contracts, dated 25 December 1414 and confirmed by the councils of the people and Comune of Florence; ordinary customs duties (*gabelle ordinarie*) and passage-tolls are expressly excluded from the exemption. It reflects Florentine commercial policy toward Pisa after Florence's acquisition of the city (1406), the kind of privilege a Frescobaldi merchant would copy for reference. Several heavily abbreviated words remain uncertain (see `[?]`).
