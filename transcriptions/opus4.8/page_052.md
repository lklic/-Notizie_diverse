**English translation.**

**✠ Damascus**

- In Damascus everything is dealt in one money, called the *dattaro* (dīnār), worth florins of gold fl. 23–24; the *dattaro* takes more or less according to the bezants in use.
- All heavy (bulk) spices are sold by a weight called the *chontaro* (qintār). The said *chontaro* converts in Venice to lb 982. The "florin" of a *tolo* (raṭl?) of the said Venice *chontaro* is lb 9, oz 10 t. By the said raṭl every fine (light) spice is sold.
- The *cottinaro* of *pingi* (ginger?), dealing in all fine goods that they sell, converts at oz 8, dl 20 in Venice.
- One sells *asarone*, figs called *ple*; seven *asaroni* are oz one good [reading uncertain] in Venice.
- One sells cloth that, [reckoning] each aretine *aro* of *pingi* at *vento* (twenty?) *pugi*, comes in Venice to a *broma* of eighty-eight.
- The *centaro* 1¼ of Damascus converts there to one good *chontaro*. Four *dattari* of Damascus are worth one bezant of *argi* (silver?).
- There are exported: sturgeon[?], cottons, abundant spices, silk cloths, golden figs, pearls, and other coarse goods.
- There are imported: cloths of Florence, of Lombardy, *almoghnardo*, linens, *chamo barni*(?), oil, honey, and almonds.
- We affirm *vesimatte* and sundry sorts [are] goods of Catalonia.
- [Imports continue:] *ovasse*, chestnut[-dye?], sulphur, quicksilver, copper, tin in bars, iron, *aprene* of Milan, *aviento ladeo*, salt, *mteo*, black, and corals.
- Damascus, when it sells off all its spices by weight, converts in Venice to lb 982 // in Genoa lb 950; the said *chontaro* is held [valid].
- Damascus, when it weighs 100 fine goods and 100 *degns*, converts the said florins-weights in Venice to oz 8, dl 20 // and in Genoa to oz 8, dl 8.
- Damascus, when sold by *asfsegio*(?), converts to lb 7 *sagi* in Venice, oz one good // and in Genoa converts to dn. 7 *sagi*, dl 22½.

**✠ Cyprus**

- In Cyprus there are two monies: white bezants (*besanti bianchi*) and saracen bezants (*besanti saracenati*).
- *Char an noto* [a coin] is worth 3 t in *aldonigi* of Cyprus [reading uncertain].
- There are exported: *dapesi*, ginger, ginger-powder, copper *de metes*, *vombollotti*, *hotoni* (cottons) spun, *borsmm*, and all spices.
- There are imported: non-fine cloths of Florence, of Lombardy, *almoghnardo*, *mervne*, *amrlomo*, and some manufactured [goods].
- The hundredweight (*centenaro*) of *argi* (silver?) converts [in Genoa? — *gen luogho... non*] in Venice lb 750 // in Cremona lb 720 // in France lb 656 // in Rome lb 631 // in Naples lb 661 // in Bologna lb 627 // in Milan lb 668 // in Avignon lb 670 // in Montpellier lb 670 // in Barcelona lb 554 // in Seville lb 590 // and in Paris lb 948.

---

**Interpretive note.** This is a two-part commercial gazetteer for the Levantine markets of Damascus and Cyprus. For each it records the local money (the *dattaro*/dīnār valued in gold florins; Cypriot white and saracen bezants), the principal spice weights (the *chontaro*/qintār and *cottinaro*) with their *ragguagli* into the Venetian and Genoese pound and ounce (e.g. the contaro = lb 982 in Venice, lb 950 in Genoa), and lists of goods exported from versus imported into each market (cottons, silk cloth, spices, sturgeon, figs and pearls against Florentine and Lombard cloth, oil, honey, almonds, metals, sulphur, quicksilver and coral). The Cyprus block closes with a long *ragguaglio* table converting the hundredweight of silver(?) into the pound of a dozen European marketplaces from Venice to Paris. Readings are uncertain throughout owing to the abraded mercantesca hand.
