**English translation.**

*(The hand is heavily abraded; readings marked `[?]` are uncertain.)*

Heading — *"This memorandum, of the wages/manning of a galley."*

Wage list of galley personnel, each with a monthly salary in florins (fl. = fiorini):
- the head of the salary roster (comito[?]), … per month, who is not to be passed over — **fl. 12**
- one already engaged, nine per month — **fl. 9**
- the man of counsel, who handles the galley… etc., per month — **fl. 10**
- a servant/factor, eight per month — **fl. 8**
- … who wishes to be a good servant … understanding (the work) — **fl. 6**
- … seven, wishes to be a manager/overseer — **fl. 7**
- the *ramolano*[?] (deck hand), eight per month — **fl. 8**
- the *maestro d'ascia* (ship's carpenter), seven per month — **fl. 7**
- the *maliscalco* (farrier/smith), seven, who is lacking[?], per month — **fl. 5**
- the under-*maliscalco*, four per month — **fl. 4**

**Interpretive note.** A *ricordo* (memorandum) of the **soldamento d'una galea** — the manning and monthly wages of a galley. The list runs through standard galley personnel (the *comito*/master and his deputy, the *uomo di consiglio*, ramolano, *maestro d'ascia* = ship's carpenter, *maliscalco* (and under-maliscalco), *calafato* = caulker, *compagnoni* = oarsmen, *prodieri* and *portolani*), each paid so many florins per month (from fl. 12 down to fl. 2½). The prose then tallies the full complement — about 60-plus and 166 oarsmen, "in tutto sono uomini 230 sanza l'apadrone" (230 men in all besides the patron) — and details the crew's victualling: ship's biscuit (buschotto/biscotto) issued by the purser (scrivano), fresh and salted provisions, cheese (formaggio), sardines (sardelle), and the daily morning ration. Heavily abbreviated and abraded; many readings remain doubtful.
