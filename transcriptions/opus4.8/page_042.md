**English translation.**

Heading: *"Summary of Florence — how it re-exchanges."* / *"Florence reckons / settles its exchange:"*

- **Pisa** re-exchanges by *conto* (account); likewise Pisa re-exchanges to Florence.
- **Siena, Lucca, Perugia** by *conto*; likewise from those places to Florence.
- **Rome** re-exchanges by *conto* of the Camera (papal chamber); and from Rome to Florence likewise.
- **Naples, Gaeta and all Puglia** re-exchange at 300 by *conto*, all the *once* of the *carlino*(?) being 98 to 90 according to [time]; the *carlino*(?) being worth 60 …; likewise re-exchange from those places to Florence.
- **Palermo and all [Sicily]** re-exchange by *conto*, being better or worse according to the times; likewise re-exchange from those places to Florence.
- **Milan**(?) re-exchanges by *conto*, of 32 *imperiali* … alum(?); likewise … to Florence.
- **[Avignon — Senione?]** re-exchanges by *conto* of the Camera; likewise from Avignon to Florence.
- **Montpellier** re-exchanges by *conto* in *franchi*(?); likewise re-exchanges from Montpellier to Florence.

**Interpretive note.** A "Rasunto di Firenze" — a city-by-city summary of how Florence conducts re-exchange (*ricambio/cambio*) with a long list of marketplaces: Pisa, Siena/Lucca/Perugia, Rome (papal Camera), Naples–Gaeta–Puglia, Palermo and Sicily, Milan, Avignon, Montpellier, Barcelona/Valencia, Paris, Bruges and London (the last in *sterlings*), each conducted "by account" (*per conto*) with rates that vary by season ("sehondo tempi"). It then gives Florentine metrology — the alloy/standard of the *popolino*, and how the Florentine pound (*libbra*) converts into the ounces of Genoa, Naples, Bologna, Avignon(?) and into 112 *pesi* at Alexandria — the *oncia* standard at Florence (10¼ oz/lb), and notes on Florentine and English coinage, English wool, cheese and spices traded at Florence. A closing "+ pisa" rubric opens a short note on goods imported through Pisa (Catalan wool, hides, Spanish leather, Barbary sugar and merchandise) and redistributed to Florence, Lombardy and the Marches.
