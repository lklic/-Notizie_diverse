**English translation.**

(Top outer corner: folio **12**.)

The upper block concerns where **silver / argento** is *bandito* (officially priced / cried) and the corresponding assayer's terms: it is "cried" at **Palermo** and **Messina**, and likewise at **Avignon** as *argentaro*; further notes on the assay name used at Palermo, Pisa and Avignon. One should bring / show cloths "of the second grade" to **Avignon** of **Florence**(?): the writer notes recognition marks and a fine "quarter" of cloths — 6 pieces, black and dark purple, 1 dark green, 1 "famous"(?) and 1 *alepri* (Aleppo-grade?). At **Rome** each *vespro*(?) ... and at **Bologna** ...; *argento doppio* (double / refined silver) converts at Florence to lb [...] 2 (per ⅌). At **Pisa** it is called *Sala di Pisa*, fl. 10–[..]; at **Palermo** it is priced 15–1[?] per thousand; the bars should be sound and refined. For **gold** ("in oro") refined(?) at tor. 24–26 *argentaro*, and at **Pisa** fl. 19¾–16 per thousand; **iron** at Pisa, fl. 16 per thousand. One should provide a consignment(?) of gilt(?) cloths for **Palermo** of good price — black and dark good purple, good dark vermilion, and *alepri*, one dark *aymero*, 2 *ferrotti*, one "famous", one black — for the whole consignment per *torone*. *Argento doppio* converts to French money(?)... and if assayed at **Pisa** they are fl. 15.

**"Salute di Spagna"** (heading) — a price list (saffron / dye / cloth grades by marketplace; oncia = ounce, tor. = tornesi, lb = libbre/soldi, ⅌ = per):

*Left column:* grade "of the coarse" at price, on 2 tor. 2; Gonzaga(?) on. 2 tor. 24; *malietto* from Monte Vibo(?) on 2 lb 20; **Toulouse (Tolosa)** on 2 lb 2; of a "vecale"(?) on 1 lb 28; of Agrimonte *chostia* on 1 lb 15; *Bie* of *aremogio*(?) on 1 lb 28; *Bie* of a "vecale" on 2 lb 12; grade "of the orborda"(?) on 1 lb 24; grade from Monte Chiso(?) on 1 lb 24; *Bie* of *scharamoscho* on 14 [lb] 16; *Bie* of *alepri* on 1 lb 24.

*Right column:* grade of **Palermo** "mare" on. 1 tor.; grade of **Castelnuovo (Castello Nuovo)** on 1 lb; "of Aleppo of the coarse" on. 2 lb 12; *alepri* close-packed, called *nominali*, on 4 lb 20 per...; *modolesi* (Modena?) close-packed, value tor. 5 on. 20...; of **London (Londra)** close-packed tor. 12–18...; "of a" close-packed, *albente*(?), on 5–6 per...; of *Abolonza*(?) on. 1 tor. 20 per piece; of **Barcelona (Barzalona)** on 1 lb 26–28 per piece; "of a long" close-packed on. 2½–3 ⅌; of **Perpignan (Perpignano)** close-packed (sono, struck through).

Closing note: At **Ragusa**, every ounce ... 6 and tor. 30 = one ounce; and every 20 grains = one *tornese*; and each *tornese* is worth d. [...] in *carlini*(?). One *cantar* of **Naples**(?) converts at **Florence** to lb 290.

**Interpretive note.** This is a working merchant's memorandum on the precious-metals and dyed-cloth trade of the western Mediterranean. The discursive upper block records how silver (and gold/iron) are officially "cried" and assayed (*argentaro*) at Palermo, Messina, Pisa and Avignon, with grade/colour specifications for cloth consignments destined for Palermo, and conversions of *argento doppio* and per-thousand prices into Florentine florins. Beneath, under the rubric "Salute di Spagna," a two-column table lists grades of (probably) saffron or dyestuff priced in ounces, soldi and tornesi across markets from Toulouse, Aleppo, Modena(?), London, Barcelona and Perpignan to Palermo and Castelnuovo. The page closes with weight/coin ratios for Ragusa and a *cantar*-to-libbre conversion into Florence (lb 290). The hand is heavily abbreviated and abraded; many place- and commodity-grade tokens are read with reservation.
