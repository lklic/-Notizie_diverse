**English translation.**

**Milan**
- From Milan [to] Paris and Bruges: two months — at the making (date drawn); thus they are paid.
- From Milan to Avignon and Montpellier: 10 days after sight; thus paid.
- From Milan to Rome: 10 days after sight; thus paid.
- From Milan to Bologna: 10 days after sight; thus paid.
- From Milan to Perugia and Siena: 10 days after sight; thus paid.

**Bologna**
- From Bologna [to] Paris and Bruges: two months at the making; thus paid.
- From Bologna to Barcelona: two months at the making; thus paid.
- From Bologna to Rome: 10 days after sight; thus paid.
- From Bologna to Perugia: 10 days after sight; thus paid.
- From Bologna to Siena[?]: 3 days after sight; thus paid.
- From Bologna to Lucca: 5 days after sight; thus paid.

**Avignon**
- From Avignon [to] Montpellier: 2 days after sight; thus paid.
- From Avignon to Paris and Bruges: one month at the making; thus paid.
- From Avignon to Barcelona: 10 days after sight; thus paid.

**Montpellier**
- From Montpellier [to] Bruges: 40 days [from] the making; thus paid.
- From Montpellier [to] Paris: one month [from] the making; thus paid.
- From Montpellier to Barcelona: 20 days after sight; thus paid.

**Paris**
- From Paris to Bruges: 10 days after sight; ⟨and from Bruges to Paris: one month at the making⟩.
- From Paris to London: one month [from] the making; thus paid.

**Bruges**
- From Bruges to Rome: one month [from] the making; thus paid.

**Barcelona**
- From Barcelona to Paris and Bruges: 30[?] days after sight; thus paid.
- From Barcelona to Avignon[?]: 30 days after sight; thus paid.

**Interpretive note.** This page is a table of *usanze* (usances) — the customary maturity terms for bills of exchange drawn between the major marketplaces, grouped by place of issue (Milan, Bologna, Avignon, Montpellier, Paris, Bruges, Barcelona). For each destination it gives the term at which the bill falls due: "dì N vista" = N days after sight, or "mesi/dì N (alla) fatta" = months/days reckoned from the date the bill was drawn, closing with the formula read "essi paboeso" (apparently "è uso paese," the local custom). One Paris entry also records the reciprocal return term. The page documents the time-structure of the late-medieval / Renaissance trans-Alpine exchange network linking Lombardy and Central Italy (Rome, Florence, Lucca, Perugia, Siena) with Avignon, Montpellier, Paris, Bruges, London and Barcelona.
